Morse Lab — Brand & Product Guidelines

Paper, ink, one spark of amber — and the rest is sound.

This document covers the entire project: brand, logo, color, typography, layout, components, iconography, motion, sound, voice, accessibility and implementation tokens. It is the reference for design and development alike. A German summary version (CI 1.0) exists; where the two differ, this document governs.

1 · Brand core

Morse Lab is a web app for learning Morse code. It treats learning as a focused ritual: nothing pushes, nothing blinks, nothing rewards with noise. The interface behaves like a good notebook — warm paper, dark ink, exactly one accent. Every design decision is tested against four principles:

  • Calm before stimulus. Generous whitespace; no shadows, no gradients, no emojis, no confetti.
  • Outlined, not filled. Thin lines carry the interface; filled surfaces are the exception.
  • One accent. Amber marks the single most important thing per view — never two things at once.
  • The rest is sound. The app makes no sound other than Morse.

Litmus test: could this screen sit quietly on a desk next to a paper notebook and a straight key without embarrassing either?

2 · Name & wordmark

  • The name is Morse Lab — two words, both capitalized. Never “MorseLab”, “morselab” or “MORSE LAB”.
  • Domain: morse-lab.com (hyphenated, lowercase); the pages.dev URL remains as fallback.
  • Wordmark: “Morse Lab” in Newsreader Regular, sentence case, default tracking. Never letterspaced, never all-caps.

3 · Logo — the key

The mark is a straight key (Morse key) reduced to three geometric elements: base, lever, knob. The knob is the only filled amber surface in the brand — the part you touch.

Construction

  • Base 120 × 8, lever 92 × 8, both radius 4 (half the bar height), ink.
  • Lever raised 13°, pivot at the right bearing.
  • Knob ⌀ 30, amber, filled. Bearing ⌀ 10, 3 px ink outline, unfilled.
  • Clear space: one knob diameter on all sides.
  • Minimum sizes: mark alone 24 px wide, lockup with wordmark 140 px. Below that: fallback mark, dot + pill (· —).

Variants

  • Primary lockup — mark above or left of the wordmark, on paper.
  • Inverse — on ink: bars in paper, knob stays amber.
  • App icon / favicon — mark alone, centered on paper, 10 px corner radius at 44 px (scale proportionally).
  • Fallback mark — dot + pill in amber, for sizes under 24 px and inline contexts.

Don’ts

Do not fill or thicken the bars · do not rotate or mirror the mark · no shadows or gradients · knob in any color other than amber · logo on an amber surface (paper or ink only) · wordmark letterspaced or in all-caps.

4 · Color

Four tones, no more. Amber is rationed: exactly one element per view.

SwatchRoleHexUsage
 Paper#F6F1E8Base surface of every view; light logo ground
 Ink#221D16Text, lines, icons; dark ground
 Amber#B45309The one accent: knob, active state, links, the element currently sounding
 Warm gray#6F6455Secondary text, labels, captions
 Paper edges#E5DCCB / #DED5C4 / #FFFCF6Hairlines, card borders, card fill — never for text
  • On ink ground: text in paper, amber stays amber. No transparency, no additional grays.
  • Contrast: body text is always ink on paper (≈ 13:1). Warm gray only for text ≥ 11 px (passes 4.5:1). Amber text ≥ 14 px or bold.
  • Amber never appears twice in one view. If two things compete, one of them is wrong.
  • No semantic red/green. Correct = a calm ink checkmark; incorrect = the correct answer shown in amber. Errors are information, not alarms.

5 · Typography

Two typefaces, never more. Newsreader (serif) for headlines, questions and the letters being learned. IBM Plex Sans for everything functional: body copy, buttons, labels, statistics. IBM Plex Mono only inside code blocks in documentation, never in the product UI.

6 · Layout & spacing

  • Spacing scale: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 px. Nothing in between.
  • Content width: max 680 px for reading, 960 px for dashboards. One column by default; the practice view is a single centered column.
  • Corner radius: 10 px on cards, inputs and buttons — one radius everywhere (circles excepted).
  • Borders: 1 px in paper-edge tones. Depth comes from lines and distance — no shadows, ever.
  • Whitespace: sections breathe; prefer a second screen over a full one. The practice letter sits alone with at least 64 px of clearance.
  • Touch targets: minimum 44 × 44 px on mobile; the key button is full-width.

7 · Components

  • Play button: an empty circle with an ink outline. It fills — amber — only while sound is playing, and empties again when it stops.
  • Buttons: outlined by default; exactly one filled amber primary per view (usually “Start” or “Check”). No disabled-gray ghost rows — hide what can’t be used.
  • Progress: a hairline 2 px track in paper-edge tone with an ink fill. Never a thick bar, a ring, or a percentage badge.
  • Cards: #FFFCF6 fill, 1 px #DED5C4 border, radius 10, padding 24. Used sparingly — most content sits directly on paper.
  • Inputs: outlined, radius 10; focus = 2 px ink outline (no glow). The answer field is the largest element after the practice letter.
  • The key button: the tap/space target for keying practice mirrors the logo — a wide outlined pill that “presses” 2 px down while held, knob-amber while sounding.
  • Statistics: plain tabular numbers with labels, separated by hairlines. No gauges, no medals, no streak flames.

8 · Iconography & the code motif

  • UI icons are line icons: 1.5 px stroke, ink, round caps, 20/24 px grid. No filled or two-tone icons.
  • The Morse code itself is the brand’s only ornament — always real, readable code in correct proportions. Base unit u = dot diameter.
  • Dot: circle ⌀ 1 u · dash: pill 3 u × 1 u, radius 0.5 u · gap within a character 1 u · between letters 3 u · between words 7 u.
  • Color: ink; the element currently sounding is amber. Decorative fake code is never allowed — if code appears, it spells something.

9 · Motion

  • Motion is functional, never celebratory. Two durations: 150 ms (state: hover, press, fill) and 250 ms (layout: panels, transitions), both ease-out.
  • The only expressive animation is the signal itself: dots and dashes turning amber in time with the sound; the play circle filling while audio runs.
  • Nothing bounces, nothing pulses idle, nothing auto-plays. Motion follows a user action or the audio clock — never a timer.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: keep the amber sync highlight (it is information), drop everything else.

10 · Sound

  • One voice: a warm 620 Hz sine tone. No square waves, no chords, no alternate pitches.
  • Envelope: soft ramps, attack/release ≈ 8 ms — no clicks, no hard cut-offs.
  • Timing follows the PARIS standard from the configured WPM; Farnsworth spacing allowed for beginners.
  • The app makes no other sound than Morse: no success jingles, no notification pings, no UI clicks. Silence is part of the brand.

11 · Voice & tone

Calm, precise, honest. No exclamation marks, no guilt. Praise sparingly and concretely, correct factually, always offer the next step. Product copy is English first; the German UI translation follows the same rules.

Like thisNot this
Correct. Next up: K.Awesome!!! You’re on fire 🎉
That was · — · · — L. Hear it again?Wrong. Try harder.
12 minutes practiced. R is waiting tomorrow.Don’t lose your streak!!!
Audio is off. Turn it on to practice by ear.Oops! Something needs your attention 😬
  • Short sentences. Present tense. Second person only when needed; often no pronoun at all.
  • Numbers are facts, not trophies: “14 of 26 letters” — never “14 letters unlocked”.
  • Errors name the fix, not the fault. Empty states say what will appear here, in one line.

12 · Accessibility

  • Contrast: ink on paper ≈ 13:1; warm gray only ≥ 11 px; amber text ≥ 14 px or bold. All pass WCAG AA in their roles.
  • Every audio exercise has a visual twin: the code lights up in sync, so deaf and hard-of-hearing users can practice by sight (and everyone can practice muted).
  • Full keyboard operation: space = key down, enter = check, letters answer directly. Focus is a 2 px ink outline, never suppressed.
  • Meaning never rides on color alone — amber highlights pair with position or motion in time.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion and prefers-color-scheme (dark mode = ink ground, paper text, amber unchanged).
